# Diagnostic Knowledge Base

> Step-by-step diagnostic guides per equipment line, split by complexity level, so Tier 1 solves what belongs to Tier 1 and escalates only what needs investigation.
>
> **Type:** technical documentation · **Format:** decision trees, guides and video walkthroughs · **My role:** author

## The problem

Tier 1 escalated cases it could have solved, because there was no clear diagnostic path. Tier 2 then started every case from scratch.

## The solution

A knowledge base covering the main equipment lines (laser cutters, fiber lasers, printing and cutting plotters, 3D printers). Every documented issue has the same four parts:

1. **Diagnosis:** questions in order to confirm the symptom.
2. **Cause:** what usually produces that symptom.
3. **Resolution:** practical steps.
4. **Escalation:** when to escalate, and to whom.

Issues are split into three levels (basic, intermediate, advanced). That split is what makes the Tier 1 / Tier 2 boundary fair: a level is only expected to solve what is documented as theirs.

It also includes:

- **Electrical and infrastructure basics** shared by all machines (AC/DC, power supply, grounding, polarity), with each statement marked as "from the source" or "inference".
- **Guided diagnostic paths** for new equipment lines.
- **Critical environment parameters**, such as operating temperature ranges.

## Outcome

The support team now follows the diagnostic tree as its reference for day-to-day failures.

## What I learned

- Separating by level is what lets you hold each tier accountable fairly.
- Marking what is fact and what is inference makes people trust the material.
